Two surviving directions from G1, deepened into the screens that decide it. Bench is the merged Bench plus Assembly concept: a comparison workspace fed by a parts library. Ledger is the document model. Both absorbed the three ideas worth keeping from Spec: explicit inheritance, path-addressable fields and a computed change set.

In both, hypothesis is optional, the key is generated as an ID with an override, and the naming convention is enforced on the name instead.

Where they actually separate

Four of the seven moments are effectively identical between the directions, which is a useful result: it means those decisions are settled regardless of which spine wins.

DimensionBenchLedger
Difference between variants Structural. You are always looking at it. Annotated. Accurate, but you trust the badge.
Accidental A/A Visible before you read the warning. A warning you have to read.
Four or more variants Columns compress, needs horizontal scroll. Cards stack, scales cleanly.
Building a missing package Slide-over, keeps context, narrower canvas. Full width, plans side by side, loses context.
Non-pricing experiment types Weak. A redirect test has no attribute grid. Sections vary by type without breaking the spine.
First-time comprehension The screen explains the job immediately. Familiar, but the shape of the job is less obvious.
Long experiments with many fields Grid stays fixed, rows grow. Long scroll, depends on section collapse.
Effort to build Higher. The grid is a real component. Lower. Sections and cards are conventional.

Recommendation for G2

Ledger as the spine, Bench as a view inside it

Bench wins every moment that involves reasoning about difference, and difference is the core job. But it loses on the two things that determine whether this tool survives contact with the roadmap: more than three variants, and experiment types that are not pricing. A redirect test has no attribute grid to sit in, and the session was explicit that boxing the tool in is the thing to avoid.

Ledger has the opposite profile. It scales and generalises, and it is cheaper to build, but its handling of difference is annotation rather than structure, which is exactly the weakness that made the current design frustrating.

The resolution is visible in the mid-fi itself. Ledger already has a Compare view control on the variants section, and pressing it produces Bench. So the answer is not to choose. Make the document the spine, since it generalises and scales, and make the comparison grid the default rendering of the variants section whenever there are two or three variants and the experiment type is attribute-comparable. Past three variants, or on a non-pricing type, the section falls back to stacked cards without anything else changing.

What this commits to. One navigation model, document with a contents rail and a readiness panel, no gates. One authoring surface for library objects, full-width focus mode, because that was the clearer of the two treatments. Difference computed from path-addressable fields, rendered as a grid when a grid fits and as annotation when it does not. Review as a filter, never a destination.
If you disagree. If you want the comparison grid as the permanent spine and are willing to accept that non-pricing tests get a different surface later, say so and Bench becomes primary. That is a defensible call, it just trades generality for focus.
